Duo Decks

Slide decks kept as Markdown, held to templates made from your own PowerPoint files, shown in Duo as real slides, and exported as a PowerPoint file Google Slides imports properly. This page walks through what the research found, how the pieces fit, and the four decisions that shape everything after them.

The canvas: 8 boards Director console This study's session Google Docs in Duo (related)

Nothing is built and none of it is for 0.2.22. The study is docs/design/duo-decks-study/ on branch research/duo-decks: plan.md, four research reports, samples and a working prototype. Every sample is synthetic. No work decks were used.

What you asked for

Decks as a first-class way to share context

You want to define the dozen templates you use at work, at project or user level, and build decks only from them, with Claude. You want the deck to live on disk as Markdown, show in Duo as rendered slides, and travel through git and PRs with diffs people can read. When it settles, it becomes a PowerPoint file that survives Google Slides.

You set three limits. No full PowerPoint editor. No HTML as the stored form. As little application and UI as possible, probably no WYSIWYG template editor, since most templates will come from feeding Duo existing decks.

The idea

One file, real templates, one engine

A deck is a Markdown file. Every slide names a template and fills its slots. A template is a real layout in a real PowerPoint file, and its slots are that layout's placeholders. A short note beside the pptx names each template and its slots, says what it's for, and sets limits.

Duo checks the deck against its templates. It writes the pptx by filling those placeholders, and shows you that same pptx with the slide renderer it already ships. Claude writes the Markdown and runs Duo's checker after every edit, the way a programmer runs a compiler.

The whole sample deck is 9 slides in 1,726 characters, about 430 tokens. Its exported slide XML is 22,911 characters. Boards 1 and 2 on the canvas show the system and the deck open in Duo.

What the research found

Six things that shape the plan

1

Nobody ships this combination, but every part exists. pandoc fills a company template's real placeholders, but it picks among only seven layouts and guesses which. Slidev and Obsidian's Advanced Slides name a layout per slide, but export pictures or loose text boxes. Presenton and PPTAgent turn existing decks into slot schemas. Claude's own Slides artifact type keeps a checker that names the fix and a rule: "nothing shrinks; split the slide". (F-465, research-agentic-landscape.md)

2

Anthropic's pptx skill gave up HTML-to-PowerPoint in February 2026, for masters and placeholder filling. A preview drawn one way and a file written another way drift apart, and that drift is what sank it.

3

Markdown makes the diff and token problems small. A 10-slide deck is 260 to 430 tokens of Markdown, against about 3,100 of the slimmest slide XML. A one-word edit is a one-line diff in every Markdown dialect, so the dialect barely matters. Keeping the pptx out of review does. (F-464)

4

Google Slides keeps the structure that matters. Tested in your Drive, in one folder, nothing shared: Google kept the slide's layout, the placeholders' type and number, exact positions and theme colours. It renames layouts and shapes, writes inherited formatting onto every run, and swaps fonts it lacks. A deck filled into placeholders stays editable there and can be mapped back. Loose text boxes look the same but can't be mapped back. (F-460)

5

Duo has half the machinery already. It reads pptx files in Swift and draws them with a vendored renderer. It has no writer, and there's no usable Swift one to adopt. One is about 1,000 to 2,000 lines, and it can run as a duo2 verb with Duo closed. (F-463)

6

The work Mac rules out a toolchain, not the idea. No Python, Node or LibreOffice can be counted on, but duo2 verbs are allowed by record and Claude Code 2.1.284 reads skills. So everything Claude runs is a duo2 verb. (research-duo-context.md §7)

One gap. The full Google test (ten layouts, a table, a chart, a gradient) didn't reach Drive. The Drive connector corrupts uploads over about 10 KB (F-461), so only a one-slide round trip ran. Q-355 asks to finish it another way.

Where each piece lives

Three layers

The rule: anything that must give the same answer every time belongs to the application. Anything that needs judgement or language belongs to Claude. Anything both read, and that people and other tools must read too, is a file on disk.

LayerWhat lives thereWhy there
AgentA Decks section in the duo2 skill Duo already installs. It says:
  • read duo2 deck templates, never the pptx;
  • use only those templates and slots;
  • one bullet a line, no wrapping;
  • split, never shrink;
  • run duo2 deck check after every edit and fix what it names;
  • look only when asked;
  • making templates: inventory, propose, build, show the specimen.
Choosing a template, cutting and splitting content, and naming slots are judgement. Positions, placeholder ids, theme values and the file's bytes are never trusted to Claude.
Context
  • decks/*.md, with type: deck and template: <pack>; decks/assets/.
  • templates/decks/<pack>/<pack>.pptx: masters and layouts, no slides.
  • <pack>.md: one section per template with purpose, slots and limits.
  • Lookup: the project, then Home, then one built-in pack.
Plain files in git, readable in Obsidian and on GitHub. Scope works as file templates already do (DL-146). User level means Home's folder.
Application
  • Parser, checker and writer in Swift, shared by the app and duo2.
  • The viewer: the writer's pptx drawn by the renderer Duo ships.
  • Verbs: duo2 deck templates · check · export · render, deck template inventory · build · specimen.
Deterministic, offline, and run on the work Mac. Small, because layout lives in the template file and judgement lives with Claude.

Not built, on purpose: a slide editor, a template editor, layout logic, model calls. Templates are edited where they came from (PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides) and rebuilt with one verb. Board 5 shows a template made from an existing deck in four steps.

The decisions

Four choices, in the order they bind

Each has a recommendation first. Pick one, add a note if you like, and save; your answers are stored on this page for the director. Your picks become DL-267 to DL-270.

Q-349 → DL-268

What is a template?

This binds the most. It decides the export engine, how well Google keeps the deck, and whether Duo ever needs a template editor. Products that keep their own geometry (Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Pitch) lose fidelity on export.

Q-351 → DL-270

How is the PowerPoint written, and how does it reach Google?

Once a writer exists, the viewer, the checker's fit test and Google upload all build on it. Google upload depends on the Google Docs work's sign-in decision (Checkpoint 1, still open), so it shouldn't hold the first slice.

Q-350 → DL-269

What do you see when you open a deck?

Option A needs the writer in the first slice. In return, the preview can never disagree with the file. Board 8 sketches all four. The chosen one gets a full design round before it's built.

Q-348 → DL-267

How is a deck written?

This fixes the parser, the skill's wording and how decks read on GitHub and in Obsidian. It's the easiest to change later, since a converter between two conventions is small, but every deck written before a change needs converting.

  1. The template model decides the writer, Google fidelity and whether Duo needs a template editor. It's the hardest to undo.
  2. The viewer: drawing the export means the writer comes first. A separate preview means building every template feature twice, and keeping both in step.
  3. The writer is what everything later builds on: the fit check, the picker naming slots, Google upload.
  4. The source format is the cheapest to change, because the parser is deterministic. Settle it before decks pile up.
  5. Google upload waits on Duo's Google sign-in. Nothing above waits on it.
Phases

Each one usable on its own

P0
Spike. The Swift writer turns the Northwind pack and the sample deck into a pptx that Duo's renderer and LibreOffice both draw. The full Google test. Settles the font policy. Nothing visible.
P1
Write a deck, see it, export it.
  • The convention, parser and checker.
  • duo2 deck templates · check · export.
  • The skill section and the built-in pack.
  • A deck opens as source beside its slides.
A pack whose layouts already have placeholders works from a hand-written definition. This is the first useful slice: write a deck with Claude, read it as slides, review it in a PR, hand a pptx to Google.
P2
Templates from your decks. Inventory, build (turning slide-only templates into layouts), specimen. A fit check measured against each placeholder's real box. This is how most templates will arrive.
P3
The viewer earns its keep. Designed first, then built.
  • Click a slide's text to go to its line.
  • Select Shape names the slot, and Send to Claude says which.
  • Problems show on their slides.
  • duo2 deck diff lists changed slides.
  • Pictures dropped onto a slide land in assets/.
P4
To Google. Upload from Duo, once Duo's Google sign-in exists.
Later
Typing on a slide (ENH-296). Bringing a deck back from Google (ENH-297). Cloning a sample slide as a template (ENH-298). Charts from a table (ENH-299). SmartArt never.
