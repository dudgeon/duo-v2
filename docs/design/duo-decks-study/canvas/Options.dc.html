Q-350, after split view: source and slides side by side · [P] sketches; the window is drawn as sidebar · console · right pane Geoff: "split view is the correct way to handle the side-by-side of source editing and rendered preview." These variants use split view; split view's own study (session split-view) frames the final choice. Every variant draws the export with the renderer Duo ships and syncs both ways: the caret's slide shows; a click on a slide finds its line.
Split view · recommended S1 · Two sides of a split in the right pane
  • The file's tab holds both sides; the divider drags
  • Formatting bar and mic over the source side only
  • The slides' bar: ‹ › Slide n, problems, Select Shape, Export ⌄
Needs split view's "same file, two views" (R1). The right pane widens to fit both; the console gives way first, as DL-129 says.
Split view S2 · Slides in a pane of their own
  • Source stays in the right pane; slides open as a further pane beside it
  • Same as doc beside doc (legacy Split View), with the second pane bound to the first
  • More room for slides
Fits if split view adds panes rather than splitting one. Two tab strips to keep in step.
Split view · narrow S3 · Stacked: slides over source
  • When the pane is too narrow for slides side by side (under about 360 pt a side)
  • The current slide on top, its source below
  • Switches from S1 on its own as the pane narrows
Split view's call on how a split folds (R7). Decks only need both sides kept reachable.
Without split view F · Source / Slides toggle in one tab
SourceSlides
  • The same deck provider, one side at a time
  • The toggle keeps your place: slide n ↔ its lines
  • Ships with no split view at all
The fallback if split view slips. Also what S1 folds to in the narrowest panes.
