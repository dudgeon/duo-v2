Question 3 · How the two tabs read in the console strip [P]
Console tabs keep the state glyphs (DL-174) and DL-225's scrolling strip. A fork's tab always goes right after the tab it came from, wherever that is in the strip.
A · names alone (recommended, not chosen)
Top, after /branch: Claude titles the branch "‹title› (Branch)", so the tabs differ by themselves. Below, after /fork: /fork writes no title, so Duo shows "‹title› (Fork)" until Claude names the fork (a stand-in, mirroring Claude's own word). Nothing else is added to a tab; titles still cap at 24 characters, cutting the end, so a long name keeps its "(Br…".
B · a fork mark · chosen (Geoff, 2026-10-20)
A drawn fork mark (10 × 10, stroke 1.3, round caps, in the tab's text colour: consoleText2, consoleText when selected) after the state glyph, 6 before the title, on every fork's tab for as long as it is open. The title is the session's own: Claude's "(Branch)" for a /branch (top), the parent's title for a /fork until Claude renames it (below). It survives a rename. The original's tab has no mark.
C · the parent's name unchanged
The fork's tab takes whatever name the session has: for a /fork, the same as the original's. Two identical tabs side by side, until Claude retitles one.
Many tabs (any option)
The new tab goes right after its original, not at the end. After /branch it is selected, so the strip scrolls it into view with 32 of its neighbour (DL-225). After /fork the original stays selected and the strip doesn't move; if the fork's tab lands out of view, it is in the all-tabs menu (n ⌄). The light strip over chat (DL-136) follows the same rules.
