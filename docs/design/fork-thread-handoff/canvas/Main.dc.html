Forks made in the TUI: the thread row, the two tabs, the moment after

GitHub #14 and DL-227 (option A): a /branch or /fork in a Duo session leaves both sessions live, each in its own tab, and the left pane shows them as one thread. This canvas draws what DL-227 left undrawn. Everything on boards 1–5 is a proposal, marked [P]. Sample data only.
Today, after a /branch (as built)
The original vanishes. The tab re-keys to the branch, so the original has no tab and no process; it folds into a thread row that shows only the most urgent member's name ("thread · 2"). Its task links don't carry over. A /fork keeps the tab, but the fork lands in "open somewhere else" (DL-109).
Option A, chosen (DL-227)
  • /branch: the live terminal becomes the branch in a new tab right of the original, and keeps focus. The original reopens in its own tab by claude --resume, at its prompt.
  • /fork: a new tab right of the original attaches to the fork (claude attach). Focus stays on the original: /fork means "keep working here".
  • The fork joins every task the original is in (DL-116).
  • The left pane shows both as one thread, in the DL-217 order: PINNED · NEEDS YOU · OPEN · TASKS · OTHER SESSIONS.
  • ⌘W on an attached fork detaches; the fork keeps running in Claude's background.
  • Unchanged: /clear and /resume keep today's behaviour; DL-174's glyphs; console tabs keep the state glyphs.

The questions · answered by Geoff, 2026-10-20: A, A, B, A
  1. The expanded thread (board 1). A: the original leads, its forks hang under the thread rule, as a task's sessions do; folded, the row keeps the original's name. B: a thread heading over all its members. C: forks on two lines, "Fork of ‹name› · 2m ago".
  2. When a thread opens itself (board 1). A: it opens when a fork is made in Duo, then stays as you leave it. B: open whenever two or more of its sessions have tabs. C: as built, folded until clicked.
  3. The fork's tab (board 4). Chosen: B, a fork mark after the state glyph, with the session's own title. Not chosen: A, names alone with "‹name› (Fork)" (recommended); C, the parent's name unchanged and no mark.
  4. A thread split across sections (board 5). A: it stays one row, placed by its liveliest session; only a session that needs you leaves it, tagged "Fork of ‹name›". B: each session goes to its own section, tagged.
