Underline: Duo's underlines already mean something

Markdown has no underline. Duo writes none today: no ⌘U and no <u>. In a Duo document an underline already means a link (a thin controlEdge line) or a suggested insertion (a line in the person's colour). A third kind would sit beside them.

Today · a link
See the refunds policy for limits.
See the refunds policy for limits.
Today · a suggested insertion
As finance and legal confirmed.
As finance and legal confirmed.
Would be · underline [P]
Async refunds must wait a day.
Async refunds must wait a day.

Underline drawn heavier (1.5, in text) to part it from a link. In a sentence that has all three, a reader still has to stop and work out which is which.

U1 · Leave it out (recommended) [P]

Italic takes the slot Geoff's list gave to “italic/underline”. Emphasis stays Markdown that every reader draws the same way (GitHub, Obsidian, Claude). An underline in a pasted document stays as its text. ⌘U stays free.

U2 · Underline as <u> [P]

⌘U and Format › Underline wrap the selection in <u>…</u>, drawn as above, the tags hidden off the caret's line. That's HTML inside Markdown: Obsidian draws it, and other readers vary. A new chord needs a DL (DL-34). About ½ day on top of any option.

Not offered · highlight instead

==text== is Obsidian's highlight, the usual Markdown stand-in for “mark this”. But its yellow is a comment's anchor (commentHighlight, DL-237), and == is already part of CriticMarkup's {== ==}. Two meanings again.
