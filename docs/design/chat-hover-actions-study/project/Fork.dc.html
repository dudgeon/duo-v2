Q-508 · Fork from here: a new session that starts at this turn, in a tab right after this one [P]
No sheet: a fork changes nothing in the original, so there is nothing to confirm. The new tab follows DL-228: right after its original, the fork mark after its state glyph, the session’s own title; the left pane shows the two as a thread. Both sessions share the project folder, as every fork does today, so a fork never puts files back.
A (recommended) · from your message
The fork opens focused, in chat. Its conversation ends just before your message, and your message waits in its composer to change and send: “try it another way” without losing the first way.
A · from Claude’s reply
The fork keeps everything up to and including that reply; the composer is empty. From the latest reply it’s a plain fork, as Resume as a Fork already runs.
How Duo does it (A and B)
1 claude --resume ‹this› --fork-session --session-id ‹new› (Duo mints the id, DL-14; a whole copy) 2 in the copy only, Claude’s rewind menu: the prompt after the fork point › Restore conversation (never code: both sessions share the folder)
Step 2 is skipped for the latest reply. From your message it rewinds to that message; from Claude’s reply, to your next one.
Why not one flag: Claude Code has --resume-session-at ‹uuid›, but only in print mode, which can’t open an interactive session. Duo doesn’t write a cut copy of the transcript itself.
If step 2 can’t finish, the fork shows its terminal with Claude’s menu open, as board 2. The fork already exists and is whole, so nothing is lost; the original is never touched.
The fork’s name: Claude titles it as its parent; the tab shows that title with the fork mark (DL-228). The left pane’s thread row says “Fork of Refund flows”.
B · the same, opened behind
You stay in the original; the new tab appears after it and a note says so. Good for “save this point and carry on”; worse for “try another way”, which is why you’d fork from your own message.
C · only from the latest reply
Fork shows only on the latest reply: a plain whole fork, no rewind step, no risk. Earlier turns get Copy and Rewind only. The cheapest build; it isn’t “fork from here”.
While Claude works Fork waits (board 1): the transcript’s last turn isn’t finished, and Claude’s fork would copy a half turn.
A fork of a fork works the same; the thread row lists it under the same original.
Task links: the fork joins the original’s tasks, as a /branch does (DL-227).
