DECISION 4a′ · Q-441 · ROUND 2, DIRECTION C OF 4a″ · SUPERSEDES 4a · ENH-319 · DL-312 Twelve window colours, light and dark [P] Geoff: “colors are meant to help rapidly distinguish between disparate windows”, and on 4a″ “c is good; the order should be all light, then all dark”. Six hues spread round the wheel, leaving out only the needs-you orange (h 22–64): Coral, Ochre, Green, Teal, Blue and Purple, each in a light and a deep step. The toolbar takes the colour at real chroma and its text moves from text2 to text. The sidebar, tab strips, right pane, chat and tool output take a pale tint of the same hue. The console, cards, fields, the document's page, the selected row, text and needs-you never change, and the needs-you chip keeps a solid fill on every coloured toolbar. Closest toolbars: 8.3 light (Coral, Ochre), 7.3 dark; 71% of pairs in light and 70% in dark are 15 or more apart (OKLab ΔE ×100). On the first 4a′, no pair was.

How it is derived, and how to add more Hues 20° (Coral), 75° (Ochre), 135° (Green), 185° (Teal), 250° (Blue), 315° (Purple). They were picked by a search over every in-sRGB colour for the largest smallest distance between any two toolbars. Each surface takes its step's L, and chroma up to the cap or 92% of what sRGB holds at that L and hue, whichever is less. Twelve can't all be 15 apart under any design (the best possible worst pair is about 8), so the project name in each toolbar is the second cue. To add colours, add a third step between light and deep before adding hues, then re-run docs/design/multi-window-studies/palette/window_palette.py.
